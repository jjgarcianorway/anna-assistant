[2m│ Testing[0m
[2m│ [0m

[2m│ [0m
[2m│ $ osstest[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ You should be able to hear music during the test process. If there is no audio, try to adjust the volume or refer to the #Troubleshooting section.[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ If you want to hear sounds from more than one application simultaneously, you need vmix, OSS's software mixer.[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ Check that vmix is enabled by running:[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ $ ossmix -a | grep -i vmix[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ You should see a line like vmix0-enable ON|OFF (currently ON). If you do not see any lines beginning with vmix, it probably means that vmix has not been attached to your sound device. To attach vmix, issue the command:[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ $ vmixctl attach device[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ where device is your sound device, e.g. /dev/oss/oss_envy240/pcm0.[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ To avoid having to issue this command manually in the future, you can add it to /usr/lib/oss/soundon.user, as suggested by the official documentation.[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ If you get a "Device or resource busy" error, you need to add vmix_no_autoattach=1 to /usr/lib/oss/conf/osscore.conf and then reboot.[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ See which devices are detected by running:[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ $ ossinfo[0m
[2m│ [0m
[2m│ You should be able to see your devices listed under Device Objects or Audio Devices. If the device that you want to use is not at the top of one of these sections, you have to edit /usr/lib/oss/etc/installed_drivers and place the driver for your device at the very top. It may be required to restart OSS:[0m
